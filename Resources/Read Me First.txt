Looksee
=======

Look at a monitor and keyboard focus (and the pointer) jump to the window
you last used there. Runs on the Mac's camera. Nothing is stored or sent
anywhere; frames are analysed in memory and thrown away.

Needs macOS 14 or later and at least two displays.


INSTALL (five minutes, once)

1. Drag Looksee onto the Applications folder next to it. Open it from
   Applications, not from this window.

2. macOS blocks apps that aren't from the App Store or a registered
   developer. The first time it says it "could not verify" Looksee:
     - Click Done (not Move to Bin).
     - Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down to the
       Security section, click "Open Anyway" next to Looksee, then confirm
       with your password.
     - On macOS 14 Sonoma: Control-click Looksee > Open > Open instead.

3. Allow the Camera when asked.

4. Allow Accessibility: System Settings > Privacy & Security >
   Accessibility, turn on Looksee. This is how it sees and raises other
   apps' windows.

5. Click the eyes icon in the menu bar (top right) > Calibrate Screens.
   Each screen lights up green in turn. Face it, hold still for two
   seconds, done.


USING IT

- Look at a screen. After about half a second focus lands on the window
  you last used there, and the pointer comes along.
- Shift+Cmd+G pauses and resumes. Paused means the camera is off.
- It holds still while you're typing or dragging, so reading from one
  screen while typing on another doesn't move anything.
- With one display connected it idles with the camera off.
- The menu shows what it is doing and has the options: pointer following,
  hold while typing, dwell time, launch at login, camera choice.

If it ever feels confused between two screens, run Calibrate Screens
again and turn your head and eyes to each screen the way you normally do.
